# Speakers Domain Model

- **Speaker registry** (`registry/`): The app's speaker list, outside React: one record per device id holding who and where the speaker is, its **speaker radio** and, while reached, its live state. It finds speakers (remembered ones and the network scan), keeps their connections, checks their radio, and remembers the list. Screens read it with `useSpeakers()` / `useSpeaker(id)` and send commands with `useSpeakerCommands()`.
- **BoseSpeaker**: A listed speaker as the screens show it (`registry/types.ts`): the registry's record with its live state flattened and its speaker radio as a **radio status**.
- **Speaker radio**: How a speaker gets internet radio, as read from it (`SpeakerRadio`): its speaker's server, its `margeURL`, whether it's paired, and its station sources. Facts only, so it's remembered and judged again when our server changes. Checked as soon as the speaker is reached (alongside its live connection, from the `/info` already read), again when the app comes back to the foreground, when the speaker says its info or sources changed or reports another `margeURL`, when our server changes, after a setup, when the user scans again (pull to refresh), and when "Play on…" opens with a speaker whose check wasn't answered; the last result is kept while a check runs or fails.
- **Radio status**: What the speaker radio means for the screens (`radioStatus`, `RadioStatus`): `checking` until a check first answers, `unreachable` if the speaker didn't answer it, else `ready` or `needsSetup`, with the server it plays through (none when it's ours) and its **setup offer**. Screens read only this; `stationFit` tells from it what a speaker can do with a station.
- **Setup offer**: What setting a speaker up for our server does, from its server's setup policy and whether it's on our server: set it up, set it up again (paired on ours already), only after warning that it leaves its server, or never.
- **Known speaker**: A speaker the phone has reached before, remembered with its name, model, last address and last speaker radio (`known-speakers.ts`). On launch it's listed right away as **connecting**, with that radio, tried at its last address while the network scan runs, and shown **offline** if it doesn't answer in time and the scan doesn't find it; **online** once reached. Known speakers stay listed until the user forgets them. Commands only go to online speakers (`online`); home lists all of them (`listed`).
- **Local Network intro**: On iOS the first scan triggers the system's Local Network prompt, so the first launch shows a screen explaining it, and no speaker is scanned for or connected to until the user continues. iOS doesn't tell the app when access was refused; screens where no speaker answers say where to turn it on.
- **Our server**: The Revive ST server picked in Settings (`https://api.revivest.app` by default, or a self-hosted one). The app talks to it and sets speakers up for it; it's the only server the app sets speakers up for.
- **Speaker's server**: The server a speaker gets its account, presets and stations from (`speaker-radio.ts`): our server, another Revive ST server, Bose's (gone), or a third-party one such as soundcork, AfterTouch or STR. Told from its `margeURL` and, where that isn't enough, a request to the speaker or the server.
- **Server profile**: How the app knows one kind of speaker's server (`server-profiles.ts`): how to tell a speaker uses it, its name, and its **setup policy**: set up for our server as is (Bose's, or a Revive ST server), only after warning that the speaker leaves it (soundcork, AfterTouch, servers no profile knows), or never (STR, which puts its own settings back at every start). A Revive ST server that isn't ours is warned about too, judged in the setup offer since which one is ours can change. Adding a server is adding a profile. Another Revive ST server is recognised by the name its `GET /` answers.
- **Station sources**: The kinds of station a speaker plays, read from its `/sources`: Radio Browser stations (`RADIO_BROWSER`) and custom stations (`LOCAL_INTERNET_RADIO`), when the speaker reports them `READY`. A Radio Browser station plays as a custom station, from its current stream, on a speaker without Radio Browser.
- **Internet radio setup** (code: `features/configure`): Points a speaker at our server over its TAP console (port 17000): the bmx registry, stats, marge and software update addresses, then `envswitch boseurls set`, which saves them; pairs it with the household account and restarts it. Setup only finishes once the speaker, back from the restart, has contacted our server (its **last contact**, recorded by the server per device id).
- **Ready for internet radio**: The speaker's server isn't Bose's, the speaker has an account, and a station source is ready. Any other speaker shows as needing setup. A speaker whose server we don't know counts as ready too; a station its server can't play fails when it's started. Setting a speaker up again adds a missing station source only on our server.
- **Household account**: The Marge account a household's speakers are paired with, minted by our server. Its id is the secret that grants access to the household's presets, so only server-minted ids are shared between speakers. The phone remembers one per server.
