# Speakers Domain Model

- **Speaker registry** (`registry/`): The app's speaker list, outside React: one record per device id holding who and where the speaker is, its **speaker radio** and, while reached, its live state. It finds speakers (remembered ones and the network scan), keeps their connections, checks their radio, and remembers the list. It lists speakers by name, whichever answered first. Screens read it with `useSpeakers()` / `useSpeaker(id)` and send commands with `useSpeakerCommands()`.
- **Selected speaker**: The speaker the mini player, the Home presets shelf and Now Playing control (`lib/selection.ts`, `resolveSelectedSpeaker`): the one the user last acted on (opened, played or paused from its card, turned on, played a station on, swiped to in the mini player, or picked in the speaker picker), remembered across launches. It stays selected while offline, and never changes because another speaker starts playing. Before any pick, or once it's forgotten: the first speaker playing, else the first paused with something loaded, else the first reached.
- **BoseSpeaker**: A listed speaker as the screens show it (`registry/types.ts`): the registry's record with its live state flattened and its speaker radio as a **radio status**.
- **Speaker radio**: How a speaker gets internet radio, as read from it (`SpeakerRadio`): its speaker's server, its `margeURL`, whether it's paired, and its station sources. Facts only, so it's remembered and judged again when our server changes. Checked as soon as the speaker is reached (alongside its live connection, from the `/info` already read), again when the app comes back to the foreground, when the speaker says its info or sources changed or reports another `margeURL`, when our server changes, after a setup, when the user scans again (pull to refresh), and when "Play on…" opens with a speaker whose check wasn't answered; the last result is kept while a check runs or fails.
- **Radio status**: What the speaker radio means for the screens (`radioStatus`, `RadioStatus`): `checking` until a check first answers, `unreachable` if the speaker didn't answer it, else `ready` or `needsSetup`, with the server it plays through (none when it's ours) and its **setup offer**. Screens read only this; `stationFit` tells from it what a speaker can do with a station.
- **Setup offer**: What setting a speaker up for our server does, from its server's setup policy and whether it's on our server: set it up, set it up again (paired on ours already), only after warning that it leaves its server, or never.
- **Known speaker**: A speaker the phone has reached before, remembered with its name, model, last address and last speaker radio (`known-speakers.ts`). On launch it's listed right away as **connecting**, with that radio, tried at its last address while the network scan runs, and shown **offline** if it doesn't answer in time and the scan doesn't find it; **online** once reached. Known speakers stay listed until the user forgets them. Commands only go to online speakers (`online`); home lists all of them (`listed`).
- **Stereo pair** (`lib/stereo-pair.ts`, `features/stereo-pair`): Two SoundTouch 10s the firmware groups to play as one speaker, the left one the **master** and the right one its **partner**; not a multiroom zone. Listed as one speaker, keyed by the master and named after the pair, also while connecting or offline, since known speakers remember the pair; its card, the selected speaker, the presets shelf and Now Playing go to the master, and volume, power and source set on either speaker apply to both. Made from an ST10's settings: both speakers reached, able to play in stereo, on our server with the same household account, in no pair and no zone, and our server new enough to store pairs. Pairing asks the master alone (`/addGroup`) and finishes once both speakers report the pair; a pair only one of them reports is undone. Renamed through the master; unpaired through the master, or the partner when the master isn't reached, which clears both. `GROUP_ERROR` means the partner is unreachable: the master goes to standby about 30 seconds later.
- **Partner**: The right speaker of a stereo pair. Hidden from the speaker list (`listed`, `online`) but still reached (`all`, `useSpeaker(id)`), with its own settings that link to the pair's; selecting it selects its master.
- **Local Network intro**: On iOS the first scan triggers the system's Local Network prompt, so the first launch shows a screen explaining it, and no speaker is scanned for or connected to until the user continues. iOS doesn't tell the app when access was refused; screens where no speaker answers say where to turn it on.
- **Our server**: The Revive ST server picked in Settings (`https://api.revivest.app` by default, or a self-hosted one). The app talks to it and sets speakers up for it; it's the only server the app sets speakers up for. It's released apart from the app, so the app only takes one whose `GET /` reports at least `MIN_SERVER_VERSION`: checked when it's picked, before each setup and before each stereo pair.
- **Speaker's server**: The server a speaker gets its account, presets and stations from (`SpeakerServer`, `server-profiles.ts`): our server, another Revive ST server, Bose's (gone), or a third-party one such as soundcork, AfterTouch or STR. Told from its `margeURL` and, where that isn't enough, a request to the speaker or the server.
- **Server profile**: How the app knows one kind of speaker's server (`server-profiles.ts`): how to tell a speaker uses it, its name, and its **setup policy**: set up for our server as is (Bose's, or a Revive ST server), only after warning that the speaker leaves it (soundcork, AfterTouch, servers no profile knows), or never (STR, which puts its own settings back at every start). A Revive ST server that isn't ours is warned about too, judged in the setup offer since which one is ours can change. Adding a server is adding a profile. Another Revive ST server is recognised by the name its `GET /` answers.
- **Station sources**: The kinds of station a speaker plays, read from its `/sources`: Radio Browser stations (`RADIO_BROWSER`) and custom stations (`LOCAL_INTERNET_RADIO`), when the speaker reports them `READY`. A Radio Browser station plays as a custom station, from its current stream, on a speaker without Radio Browser.
- **Internet radio setup** (code: `features/configure`): Points a speaker at our server over its TAP console (port 17000): the bmx registry, stats, marge and software update addresses, then `envswitch boseurls set`, which saves them; pairs it with the household account and restarts it. Setup only finishes once the speaker, back from the restart, has contacted our server (its **last contact**, recorded by the server per device id).
- **Radio choice** (`features/configure/radio-choice.ts`): Right after Wi-Fi setup, before internet radio setup, the app asks how the speaker should get radio: the hosted Revive ST server, the user's own Revive ST server (checked like in Settings), or keep its current server ("Not now" when that server gives it no radio, as Bose's; offered once its radio is checked). The first two become our server, for every speaker the app sets up from then on; speakers already set up keep theirs. When the speaker already plays radio through one of the first two, that option is marked current and picking it keeps the speaker as it is, with no separate keep option. Until the user picks, the selection follows the speaker's radio: what it already plays through, else the server the app is set to.
- **Ready for internet radio**: The speaker's server isn't Bose's, the speaker has an account, and a station source is ready. Any other speaker shows as needing setup. A speaker whose server we don't know is judged on the same terms; a station its server can't play fails when it's started. Setting a speaker up again adds a missing station source only on our server.
- **Household account**: The Marge account a household's speakers are paired with on our server: a random UUID the app makes (`expo-crypto`'s `randomUUID`) when the household has none yet. Its id is the secret that grants access to the household's presets, so only random ids are shared between speakers. The phone remembers one per server.
